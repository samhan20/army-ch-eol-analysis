Survey Comparison Narrative

1. Experience Comparison

Provided End of Life Care (EOLC):
Pre-Survey: 40.8% had EOLC experience
Post-Survey: 64.6% had EOLC experience
Change: +23.8%
Interpretation: There was a substantial increase in the proportion of respondents with EOLC experience after the intervention or over time.

Been in Combat Situations:
Pre-Survey: 30.6% had combat experience
Post-Survey: 29.2% had combat experience
Change: -1.4%
Interpretation: The proportion of respondents with combat experience remained essentially unchanged.

Worked with Cadavers:
Pre-Survey: 55.1% had worked with cadavers
Post-Survey: 87.5% had worked with cadavers
Change: +32.4%
Interpretation: There was a large increase in cadaver experience, suggesting more exposure to this aspect of care.

2. Preparedness Comparison

Q1. Prepared to provide end of life care:
Pre-Survey Mean: 0.96
Post-Survey Mean: 1.10
Change: +0.14
Interpretation: There was a modest increase in self-reported preparedness to provide EOL care.

Q3. Prepared to handle >10 situations per day:
Pre-Survey Mean: 0.27
Post-Survey Mean: 0.50
Change: +0.23
Interpretation: Respondents felt more prepared to handle a high volume of EOL cases after the intervention.

3. Scenario Response Comparison

S1 Q2. Will you tell SPC Smith he is going to die?
Pre-Survey Mean: 0.18
Post-Survey Mean: -0.04
Change: -0.23
Interpretation: Respondents became slightly less likely to answer “yes” to directly telling a dying soldier about their prognosis.

S2 Q2. Will you tell SGT Tanner that Jimmy is dead?
Pre-Survey Mean: -0.10
Post-Survey Mean: -0.46
Change: -0.36
Interpretation: There was a shift toward less willingness to disclose a teammate’s death in the moment.

S3 Q2. Will you pray for a foreign soldier at the medic’s request?
Pre-Survey Mean: 0.43
Post-Survey Mean: 0.73
Change: +0.30
Interpretation: Respondents became more willing to provide spiritual support for foreign soldiers at the request of medical staff.

S4 Q2. Will you pray for a Muslim soldier per his request?
Pre-Survey Mean: 1.00
Post-Survey Mean: 0.06
Change: -0.94
Interpretation: There was a marked decrease in willingness to pray a Muslim prayer per the dying soldier’s request, which may reflect increased awareness of religious boundaries or personal comfort levels.

Overall Insights
The training or experience between surveys led to more respondents having direct EOLC and cadaver experience.
Preparedness for EOLC and high-volume situations improved, but only modestly.
Scenario-based responses show nuanced changes: more willingness to provide spiritual support in some contexts, but less directness in communication about death and less willingness to perform religious rites outside one's own tradition.
