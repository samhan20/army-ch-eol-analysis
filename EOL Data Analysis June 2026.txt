EOL Data Analysis June 2026
============================

End of Life Training Survey Analysis

Analysis of 379 responses, 200 pre-training and 179 post-training.
Questions analyzed:
- Q1 — Prior end-of-life (EOL) care experience
- Q2 — Combat experience
- Q3 — Cadaver experience
- Q4 — Years serving as Chaplain/RAS
- Q1.1 — Preparedness to provide EOL care (Likert, roughly -2 to +3)
- Q1.3 — Preparedness to handle 10+ similar situations per day (same scale)

Pre/post comparisons use independent samples (different respondents at each time point). For Q1.1 and Q1.3, Mann-Whitney U tests were used because the data are ordinal Likert scores.


OVERALL PRE VS. POST TRAINING
-----------------------------

Background / Experience (Q1-Q4)

Q1 — Provided EOL care
  Pre:  42.5% Yes (85/200)
  Post: 63.7% Yes (114/179)
  Change: +21.2 percentage points (p < .001)

Q2 — Combat experience
  Pre:  33.0% Yes (66/200)
  Post: 25.7% Yes (46/179)
  Change: -7.3 percentage points (p = .149, not significant)

Q3 — Cadaver experience
  Pre:  51.0% Yes (102/200)
  Post: 70.9% Yes (127/179)
  Change: +19.9 percentage points (p < .001)

Q4 — Years serving
  Pre:  M = 5.2, SD = 4.9, Median = 4.0 (n = 151)
  Post: M = 5.6, SD = 5.3, Median = 4.0 (n = 171)
  Change: No meaningful shift (p = .442)

Pre and post groups were similar in tenure (Q4) and combat exposure (Q2). Reported EOL care (Q1) and cadaver experience (Q3) were higher post-training. That may reflect training content, recall, or self-reporting rather than a true change in lifetime experience—worth noting when interpreting.


Preparedness (Q1.1 and Q1.3)

Q1.1 — Prepared for EOL care
  Pre:  M = 0.72, SD = 1.13, Median = 1.0
  Post: M = 1.16, SD = 0.70, Median = 1.0
  p-value: 0.0002

Q1.3 — Prepared for sustained high volume
  Pre:  M = 0.10, SD = 1.05, Median = 0.0
  Post: M = 0.84, SD = 0.90, Median = 1.0
  p-value: < 0.0001

Both preparedness items improved significantly overall.

Q1.1 (single-case preparedness):
  Pre:  66.5% rated >= 1 (somewhat/agree prepared), 20.0% neutral (0), 13.5% negative (< 0)
  Post: 87.2% >= 1, 11.2% neutral, 1.7% negative

Q1.3 (sustained-volume preparedness):
  Pre:  37.0% >= 1, 36.0% neutral, 27.0% negative
  Post: 72.1% >= 1, 19.6% neutral, 8.4% negative

The largest gain was on Q1.3: fewer neutral/negative responses and a clear shift toward positive preparedness after training.


BY TRAINING PERIOD (cohorts with both pre and post data)
--------------------------------------------------------

Six dates had matched pre/post collections:

Apr 17, 2025 (Pre n=49, Post n=48)
  Q1.1: 0.96 -> 1.10 (p = .563)
  Q1.3: 0.27 -> 0.50 (p = .193)

May 29, 2025 (Pre n=47, Post n=31)
  Q1.1: 1.00 -> 1.32 (p = .127)
  Q1.3: -0.06 -> 1.19 (p < .001)

Mar 26, 2026 (Pre n=18, Post n=16)
  Q1.1: 0.67 -> 1.06 (p = .312)
  Q1.3: 0.39 -> 0.56 (p = .560)

Apr 27, 2026 (Pre n=18, Post n=13)
  Q1.1: 0.67 -> 0.85 (p = .715)
  Q1.3: 0.39 -> 0.62 (p = .513)

Apr 28, 2026 (Pre n=10, Post n=17)
  Q1.1: -0.40 -> 1.41 (p = .003)
  Q1.3: -0.30 -> 0.88 (p = .025)

Jun 1, 2026 (Pre n=20, Post n=24)
  Q1.1: 0.60 -> 1.12 (p = .087)
  Q1.3: 0.30 -> 0.71 (p = .070)

Cohort patterns:

- Apr 17, 2025 (largest early cohort): Modest gains on Q1.1 and Q1.3; neither significant. Q3 cadaver reporting rose sharply (55% -> 88% pre to post).

- May 29, 2025: Strong, significant gain on Q1.3 (near zero pre to 1.19 post). Q1.1 trended up but did not reach significance.

- Mar-Apr 2026 (smaller cohorts): Mixed results; Apr 28 showed the clearest significant improvements on both Q1.1 and Q1.3, with pre means below zero (more negative preparedness) shifting to strongly positive post scores.

- Jun 1, 2026: Meaningful gains on both items (Q1.1 p = .087; Q1.3 p = .070), near conventional significance with n = 20/24.

Background items by cohort were generally stable on Q2 and Q4. Q1 and Q3 often increased post-training (e.g., Jun 1: Q1 40% -> 83%; Q3 50% -> 71%), similar to the overall pattern.


NARRATIVE SUMMARY
-----------------

End-of-life training appears to increase self-reported preparedness, especially for handling many cases over time (Q1.3). Overall, both preparedness items improved significantly:

- Q1.1: p = 0.0002
- Q1.3: p = < 0.0001

Effects were strongest in the pooled sample and variable by cohort. Smaller or already somewhat prepared groups (e.g., Apr 2025, May 2025 on Q1.1) showed smaller or non-significant shifts. Cohorts starting with lower baseline preparedness (notably Apr 28, 2026) showed the clearest session-level gains.

Experience items (Q1-Q3) suggest post-training groups report more EOL and cadaver exposure; Q2 and Q4 did not differ meaningfully pre vs. post, supporting comparability of groups on combat history and tenure.

Limitations: Independent pre/post samples (not paired within individuals), uneven cohort sizes, and some dates with only pre or only post responses. Mann-Whitney U treats Likert scores as ordinal, which is standard but does not assume interval-level measurement.
